# Pricing as One Slider

## Form
A pricing section staged as a single control -- a slider or stepper over the one quantity that actually drives cost -- whose position rewrites a plain-language contract beneath it in real time: what you get, what it costs, what changes at the next threshold.

## Spark
You drag to 40 seats and the sentence below rewrites itself to the price, the support tier flips at 50 and tells you so before you get there, and you never compare three columns of ticks.

## Grammar
- Staging/hierarchy: the control owns the region at full width; the rewritten contract sentence is the largest text; thresholds are marked on the control; the one action sits at the end of the sentence.
- Sequence/attention: move the control, read the sentence change, notice the next threshold, act; no plan columns compete for the eye.
- Controls/state: the slider is keyboard-steppable with the value announced; the sentence updates live; states are below a threshold, at a threshold, over the top (contact sales named plainly); prices are real numbers or the region says "quote" honestly.
- Adaptation: phones keep the control full width with the sentence below and thresholds as ticks; reduced motion swaps the sentence without animation; print renders the sentence at a default position plus the threshold table.

## Web leverage
A range input bound to a pricing function that renders a templated sentence and threshold markers, with the same function exposed as a table for print and assistive fallback.

## Fits
Landing and pricing regions for usage-priced products with one dominant variable (seats, events, storage). The trap: pricing with several independent variables or plans that differ in kind, where one slider lies, and any use of invented prices or tiers that do not exist.
