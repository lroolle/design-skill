    Why the board is one screen

    A berth plan is read, not browsed. The pilot on the morning shift scans about forty rows to find the two that changed overnight, so the table keeps every row visible and puts the summary above the data, never instead of it. Density here is a feature.

    Times render in HH:MM with tabular numerals so columns stay aligned as values change; the vessel register opens in a side panel rather than a modal, because the planner usually needs both at once.

    • Arrivals and departures share one timeline.
    • Crane windows are drawn as bars, not listed as text.
    • Every change carries who made it and when.
    The plan is a promise to a ship that is still at sea. Make it legible before you make it pretty.
