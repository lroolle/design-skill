# patent sheet -- one ink on white bond

Provenance: a USPTO utility patent drawing sheet, 1960s, line art in
india ink on white bond, reference numerals, section hatching. Set by
eye against a scan and a paper copy.

## The irregularity

There is one ink. Everything is that ink at full strength or as
hatching -- gray is *lines*, not a lighter color. The paper is a bond
white with a faint warm cast (95), never blue-white. The only second
color is the examiner's red pencil on an annotated copy, and it is a
pencil: soft, grainy, warm red (25), never a fill.

## Inks

```
bond         oklch(0.975 0.006 95)      --bg
bond toned   oklch(0.950 0.006 95)      --surface
bond screen  oklch(0.920 0.007 92)      --surface-2
india ink    oklch(0.190 0.008 80)      --fg
hatch 50%    oklch(0.470 0.008 80)      --fg-2         (in the real thing, this is hatching)
hatch 25%    oklch(0.560 0.006 80)      --fg-3
rule         oklch(0.800 0.006 85)      --line
rule heavy   oklch(0.190 0.008 80)      --line-strong  (the frame line is full ink)
red pencil   oklch(0.520 0.150 25)      --accent
red soft     oklch(0.940 0.030 28)      --accent-soft
```

Elevation: none. Shadows: none. A modal is a second sheet with a
full-ink frame.

## Must not become

Broadsheet costume: a patent sheet has figure numbers and reference
numerals because they refer to things; on content with nothing to
reference, the numbering is decoration. And not the "minimal black
and white" reflex: the paper is warm and the ink is not #000.
